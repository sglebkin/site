Strategic Trading with Wealth Effects

Sergei Glebkin, Semyon Malamud, and Alberto Teguia

Working paper

This version: 16 November 2026

PDF

Abstract

We study asset pricing with strategic liquidity providers, general preferences, and multiple assets. We characterize regular symmetric equilibria through a first-order ordinary differential equation for each fixed portfolio composition. Under a restriction on wealth effects, a transversality condition selects at most one solution. With additively separable preferences and strictly concave utility from current consumption, cross-asset price-impact asymmetry orders assets by systemicity and measures deviations from the consumption-based capital asset pricing model (consumption-CAPM). With a traded risk-free asset, this pricing relation holds for every asset exactly when impact is symmetric. More systemic assets command larger relative price premia from illiquidity. An explicit equilibrium with Epstein–Zin preferences and unit elasticity of intertemporal substitution generates effective risk aversion that rises as consumption falls when liquidity providers' initial terminal endowment is risk-free. With risky endowments, systemicity ranks assets by their payoff-weighted exposure to states where initial holdings supply a larger share of consumption.
